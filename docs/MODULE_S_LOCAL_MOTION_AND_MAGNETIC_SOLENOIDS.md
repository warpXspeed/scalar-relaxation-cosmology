## 2. The Four Local Motion Rules

### Rule 1: Photon Propagation (Open Disturbance)
An open rotational phase disturbance propagates in a straight line by successive, clean, nearest-neighbor hand-offs. Energy and state travel at $c = \sqrt{\beta}$; no physical particle moves through the void.

### Rule 2: Electron Conduction (Closed Knot Drift)
An electron is a stable, closed 720° topological vortex knot. Inside a conductor, neighboring knots exert a phase-push, causing the knot to hop slowly from site to site (drift velocity $\sim 1\text{ mm/min}$). The knots do not stream end-to-end at light speed; the bulk power travels outside the wire as transverse Poynting shear waves in the surrounding dielectric lattice.

### Rule 3: High-Energy Ionization (Fractal Branching)
High-energy electrons force the ionization of neighboring lattice sites, leaving behind a disordered hysteresis trail. Successive hops follow the path of least resistance along these residual stress trails, producing chaotic, fractal "drunken-walk" discharge paths (e.g., lightning, spark gaps, Lichtenberg figures).

### Rule 4: Magnetism (Traveling Solenoidal Stress)
When a closed knot hops to an adjacent site, it re-centers its circulation axis. This sequential re-centering generates a persistent, traveling **solenoidal lattice stress** that moves with the knot and collapses when motion stops.
* **Magnetic Moment:** The localized solenoidal circulation loop around a single moving knot.
* **Lorentz Force:** The velocity-dependent phase-push exerted when a moving solenoidal sheath interacts with adjacent lattice stress.
* **Ampère's Law (Parallel Currents):** Two parallel currents generate aligned solenoidal sheaths that lower intermediate lattice tension, creating net attraction (or repulsion if anti-parallel).

* **Rotational Energy Storage:** Knots store excess rotational strain, continually replenished by large-scale background lattice gradients (electric field).
* **Gentle Hops (Conduction Heat):** Fractional strain loss during site-to-site hops dissipates as acoustic lattice vibrations (Joule heating).
* **Ionizing Hops (Radiation Output):** Large fractional strain dumps create transverse shear bursts (photons) and persistent residual twists that guide subsequent discharge branching.
