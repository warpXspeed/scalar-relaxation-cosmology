**Scalar Relaxation Cosmology (SRC) — Condensed Archive Snapshot**

**Core (unchanged)**  
Single substrate: φ — organized potential-energy crystal lattice.  
Visible universe = kinetic disturbances in φ.  
Particles = stable closed rotational/toroidal knots.  
Photons = open rotational disturbances.  
All propagation = nearest-neighbor sympathetic rotational hand-off.  
Gravity = long-range monopole lattice tightening.  
EM = local lattice stress (electric) + traveling solenoidal stress (magnetic).  
No dark matter/energy, no free-traveling particles through emptiness.

**Local motion rules**  
- Photon: open disturbance propagates in-line by successive clean hand-offs along ordered lattice sites. Energy travels; no persistent object.  
- Electron: closed knot. In a conductor, neighboring knots push it site-to-site (slow drift ~1 mm/min). Knots themselves do not stream end-to-end; they maintain structure. Energy is carried by open EM disturbances.  
- High-energy electron: forces ionization of neighboring sites, creating a disordered trail. Successive hops follow residual stress, producing chaotic, branching, fractal (drunken-walk) paths (lightning).  
- Transfer force ceases when electron addition/removal stops; resident knots stay in place.

**Magnetism**  
Moving closed knot re-centers its circulation at each hop → traveling solenoidal lattice stress that moves with the knot and vanishes when motion stops.  
This solenoidal stress is the magnetic field and supplies the velocity-dependent push (Lorentz force).  
Two parallel currents: collective solenoidal sheaths induce attraction (parallel) or repulsion (anti-parallel).  
Single moving electron: localized solenoidal loop = its magnetic moment.

**Energy**  
Powered by excess rotational energy stored in knots, continuously replenished by large-scale lattice stress (electric field).  
Gentle hops: tiny fractional loss → heat.  
Ionizing hops: large fractional dump → radiation + residual stress/twist that biases further branching.

**Method**  
One substrate only. Derive from nearest-neighbor rotational rules. Fit observations first; quantify later.
