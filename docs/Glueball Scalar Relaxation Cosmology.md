**How the (SRC)**

In SRC there is only one fundamental thing: the viscoelastic scalar substrate φ.  
All particles and all forces are different ways this single medium can be stressed, knotted, or rippled.

### Standard view vs SRC view

| Standard QCD                          | SRC interpretation                                      |
|---------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------|
| Gluons are fundamental force carriers | “Gluons” are short-range, high-frequency shear / dilatant modes of φ |
| Glueball = bound state of pure gluons | Glueball = a localized topological defect made mostly of those short-range dilatant modes, with very little ordinary knot structure |

### Why a glueball is natural in SRC

1. **Strong force is already emergent**  
   In the SRC unification table the strong force is identified with **dilatant hardening** — the substrate’s viscosity shoots up at high strain rates and short distances. That is exactly the regime where gluons operate in ordinary physics.

2. **Particles are defects, not fundamental objects**  
   Ordinary matter particles (electrons, quarks, protons) are stable topological knots (vortices / Hopfions) in φ.  
   A glueball is simply a different kind of defect: one whose energy is dominated by the short-range dilatant/shear stress rather than by a long-lived knot of the kind we call “matter.”

3. **“Mostly force, very little matter” is expected**  
   SRC never treats force and matter as two separate substances. Both are stress patterns in the same medium. A configuration that is ~90 % pure short-range dilatant stress and only ~10 % ordinary knot structure is therefore completely allowed — it is just a less common type of defect.

### Bottom line in SRC language

The reported X(2370) (if confirmed as a glueball) is a high-energy, short-range topological excitation of φ that is dominated by the dilatant (strong-force) response of the substrate rather than by the longer-lived knots we normally call particles.

It does not require any new fundamental fields. It is simply another allowed way the single scalar lattice can arrange its stress.
