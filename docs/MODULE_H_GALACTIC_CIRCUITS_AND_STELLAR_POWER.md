## 2. Stellar Dynamics: The Sun as a Substrate Anode

### 2.1 Resolution of the Coronal Heating Problem
The standard solar model cannot explain why the outer corona ($T \sim 10^6\text{ K}$) is orders of magnitude hotter than the underlying photosphere ($T \approx 5,800\text{ K}$), violating classical second-law thermal diffusion.

In SRC, the Sun functions as a primary **anode discharge node** along a galactic Birkeland filament:
* **Impedance Matching Boundary:** As galactic phase currents discharge into the solar surface, the local substrate impedance ($Z_0 = \sqrt{\mu/\varepsilon}$) undergoes a sharp phase-transition across the heliosphere.
* **Piezoelectric Shear Relaxation:** Current density spikes produce massive transverse shear stress. The substrate converts this mechanical stress into emergent electromagnetic fields via piezoelectric coupling:
  $$\mathbf{E} = \chi \sigma_{\text{shear}}, \quad P_{\text{heating}} = \frac{J^2}{\sigma_{\text{eff}}} + \chi (\nabla \cdot \mathbf{u})^2$$
* The extreme temperature of the corona is the direct signature of **double-layer acceleration and ohmic dissipation** occurring *above* the solar surface, not internal core conduction.

## 3. Planetary Energetics: Resolving the "Missing Core Heat" Anomaly

Standard geophysics relies on primordial accretion heat and radiogenic isotope decay ($\text{U}, \text{Th}, \text{K}$) to explain molten planetary cores. These mechanisms are numerically insufficient to account for observed core heat flows, long-term dynamo stability, or the active magnetism of small bodies like Mercury and Io.

### 3.1 Inductive Core Coupling & Ohmic Dissipation
Planets act as secondary capacitive nodes within the solar-galactic circuit:
1. **Galactic Current Modulation:** As the Solar System traverses galactic spiral arms, fluctuations in the background Birkeland current density ($\mathbf{J}_{\text{ext}}$) induce powerful eddy currents inside conductive planetary cores (iron/nickel alloys).
2. **Sustained Joule Heating ($P = I^2 R$):** Electromagnetic induction continuously heats planetary interiors from the outside in, preventing core solidification without requiring exotic isotope abundances.
3. **Anomalous Case Studies:**
   * **Mercury:** Proximity to the Sun's primary current discharge induces high eddy-current densities, maintaining its active dynamo despite its small volume.
   * **Io:** Heated primarily by intense electromagnetic shear and current-coupling within Jupiter's magnetospheric flux tube, rather than pure gravitational tidal flexing alone.
   * **Neptune vs. Uranus:** Neptune emits significantly more internal heat than Uranus because its orbital alignment intercepts active galactic current channels, whereas Uranus's $98^\circ$ axial tilt places its magnetic axis in a low-coupling "current shadow."
