Research Note: Solar Interior Constraints (Ref: Sunspot Mechanics)
1. Objective
To establish the physical boundary conditions of the solar interior by identifying the mechanical limitations of the Standard Gaseous Model, specifically regarding sunspot formation and photospheric discharge.
2. Observational Contradictions (The “Gas Ghost” Audit)
2.1 The Wilson Depression (Depth Paradox)
Observation: Sunspots are physical, three-dimensional depressions (Wilson Depression) reaching 500–1,500 km below the photosphere.
The Conflict: In a gaseous furnace heated from the center outward, a depression should reveal deeper, hotter, and brighter plasma layers.
Data Reality: Looking into a sunspot reveals a cooler (~3,500K) and darker floor.
Mechanical Conclusion: The photosphere is an electrically excited surface discharge layer (a double-layer). Sunspots are ruptures in this layer that expose the cooler, condensed substrate beneath the discharge.
2.2 Boundary Rigidity
Observation: Sunspots maintain sharp, geometrically precise transitions (umbra/penumbra) that persist across weeks and multiple solar rotations.
The Conflict: Highly compressed, turbulent gas at these scales and temperatures should exhibit rapid diffusion and lack structural integrity.
Mechanical Conclusion: Sharp boundary maintenance is indicative of condensed-state dynamics (surface tension, phase interfaces), not turbulent gas flow.
2.3 CME Compositional Markers
Observation: CMEs contain high concentrations of refractory heavy elements (Fe, Ni, Si).
The Conflict: Gaseous models require complex, ad-hoc “gravitational settling” and “fractionation” theories to explain why heavy elements are not sequestered in a deep, inaccessible core.
Mechanical Conclusion: CME material is consistent with direct mechanical ejection from a differentiated, solid metallic core interface.
3. Simulation Constraints for src/
To maintain consistency with the solid-core hypothesis, the following parameters shall govern the Sun’s behavior in the simulation:
Phase State: The solar mantle is modeled as Condensed Liquid Metallic Hydrogen overlaying a Solid Metallic/Refractory Core.
Luminosity: The photosphere is modeled as a Surface Plasma Discharge (Double-Layer), not an atmospheric gas layer.
Temperature Gradient: The Sun’s internal temperature is not a linear increase toward the core. The surface discharge layer (photosphere) is the primary site of high-energy excitation, while the interior substrate remains at a lower, condensed-state equilibrium.
Discharge Triggers: Sunspots are treated as Impedance Ruptures in the surface double-layer, causing a localized drop in potential and exposing the condensed mantle.
4. Summary for Repository Implementation
The model rejects the “Gaseous Furnace” hypothesis due to the observational evidence of cool sunspot depressions and heavy-element CME ejections. The Sun is to be simulated as a Solid/Condensed Transducer.
