## 2. Key Theoretical Distinctions & SRC Advantages

While both frameworks reject the $\Lambda\text{CDM}$ dark sector in favor of lattice mechanics, SRC maintains two distinct operational advantages:

### A. Strict Ontological Minimalism vs. Microscopic Over-Specification
* **UCBF:** Commits to a specific microscopic lattice geometry (FCC crystal of spin-0 bosons) and derived elastic constants ($C_{11} = 2C_{44}$). While predictive, this introduces model-specific assumptions that can be attacked if lattice symmetry is challenged.
* **SRC:** Remains conservatively macroscopic and continuous. It defines the substrate purely through measurable continuum mechanics ($\beta, G_{\text{shear}}, \gamma, \chi$), ensuring that the emergence of light ($c_T = \sqrt{G/\rho}$) and gravity ($c_L$) does not depend on a single microscopic crystal assumption.

### B. Redshift Mechanics & Time Dilation Integrity
* **UCBF / Older Drain Models:** Risk confusing path-dependent energy dissipation with spectral shift, creating potential conflicts with Tolman surface brightness and supernova light-curve broadening tests.
* **SRC (v2.0 Canonical):** Strictly decouples spectral shift from path loss via the **State-Rate Relation (SRR)**:
  $$1 + z = \frac{f_{\text{emit}}}{f_{\text{obs}}} = \frac{\sqrt{T_{\text{emit}}/\rho_{\text{emit}}}}{\sqrt{T_{\text{obs}}/\rho_{\text{obs}}}}$$
  This inherently satisfies $(1+z)$ supernova time dilation while confining the viscoelastic dissipation parameter ($\gamma$) strictly to **luminosity dimming**, avoiding angular blurring and FIRAS spectral distortion failure modes.
